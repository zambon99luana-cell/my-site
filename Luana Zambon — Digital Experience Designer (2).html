Hanamithe art of falling gracefully

For a few days each spring, a cherry tree stops being a tree and becomes an event.

Nothing about the blossom is permanent, and that is precisely why people gather beneath it. In Japan this quiet attention to impermanence has a name, mono no aware: a gentle sensitivity to things that pass.

I carry that idea into my work. A good layout, like a petal, does only what it needs to, and then lets go. Beauty isn’t what you add until nothing more can be added. It’s what remains once the unnecessary has fallen away.
